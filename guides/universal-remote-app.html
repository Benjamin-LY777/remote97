Overview

A universal remote app that needs no IR blaster

Most "universal remote" apps are just infrared — and most phones no longer have infrared. Remote97 takes the other route: it speaks the native network protocols your devices already expose.

8 min read · Android / Windows / iOS · pure software, no hardware

There are two very different things called a "universal remote app". One emulates an IR blaster and needs either a phone with built-in infrared or an external dongle. The other — the kind Remote97 is — talks to devices over your local network using the manufacturer's own IP control protocol. No line of sight, no hub in the middle, no monthly fee.

That distinction matters because modern AV gear is already network-connected. Your receiver, TV, streamer and multi-room speakers each expose a control endpoint; an app only has to speak it correctly.

Which devices can one app actually replace?

Remote97 discovers and controls a wide range of gear over the LAN:

  • AV receivers: Denon, Marantz (Telnet/IP), Yamaha MusicCast (YXC), Onkyo / Integra (eISCP).
  • TVs: Sony BRAVIA, Samsung Tizen, LG webOS, Xiaomi / Redmi.
  • Streamers and boxes: Apple TV, Roku, NVIDIA SHIELD.
  • Multi-room audio: Sonos, Bluesound / NAD (BluOS), WiiM, Arylic.
  • Media players: Zidoo, VidOn / Egreat, MAGNETAR UDP800 / UDP900 MKII.
  • Projectors: Epson and other PJLink Class 1/2 models (Sony, Panasonic, BenQ, JVC, Optoma, NEC, ViewSonic).
  • Cameras and integration: ONVIF IP cameras with live view and PTZ, plus RS-232 / RS-485 gear through serial-over-IP gateways.

See the full supported-device list and the complete home theater control guide for how these fit together.

Why local IP control beats a cloud hub

  • Speed — a volume command is one local TCP/HTTP round-trip, typically a few milliseconds.
  • Reliability — it keeps working when your internet provider does not.
  • Privacy — control commands stay inside your LAN instead of bouncing through someone else's server.
  • Cost — no hub to buy and no subscription for basic functions.
  • State — protocols such as eISCP and YXC report real volume and input state, unlike a one-way IR blast.

The trade-off is handled in detail in Why local IP control beats cloud hubs.

When a phone remote is NOT enough

Be realistic about power-on. Devices whose network interface sleeps in deep standby (most TVs, some players) cannot be woken by a LAN command. Keep the original remote for the first power-on, or enable your device's Network Standby / Quick Start option where available.
  • Purely infrared or RF devices with no network interface are out of scope.
  • Controlling gear from outside your home would require a cloud relay — deliberately not part of this design.
  • Vendor-specific features (lens memory, calibration) may need an RS-232 gateway instead of the standard IP protocol.
