Wake-on-LAN: wake your PC or NAS from your phone

A sleeping home theater PC, a NAS that holds your movie library, a 4K player that will not respond until it is awake — Remote97 sends the Wake-on-LAN magic packet for you, from the same app that controls the rest of your gear.

6 min read · PC / NAS / HTPC / Wake-on-LAN media players

Wake-on-LAN (WoL) is a standard way to turn on a computer over the local network. The device being woken stays in a low-power state with its network card listening. Another device on the LAN sends a small "magic packet" containing the target's MAC address, and the network card boots the machine. Remote97 turns that packet into a single wake button — and, unlike a lot of WoL tools, it drops the same action into your scenes and custom control panels.

What you can do

  • One-tap wake — send a standard 102-byte magic packet (six 0xFF bytes followed by the target MAC repeated 16 times) over UDP ports 9 and 7.
  • Both broadcast forms — a subnet-directed broadcast (for iOS, which will not deliver 255.255.255.255) plus the global broadcast as a fallback. Highest cross-platform success rate.
  • Scenes — put "wake the HTPC" at the front of a Movie night scene, followed by power/input commands to the AVR and TV. See movie night scenes.
  • Custom control panels — add a dedicated wake tile next to all your other device controls.
  • Local, private — the packet goes from your phone straight over your LAN. No hub, no cloud relay, no account, and your MAC address is never uploaded.

An honest limit: no status readback

WoL is fire-and-forget. There is no reply, so Remote97 cannot know whether the machine actually booted. The device appears as wakeable, not online/offline — we will not fake a green "online" dot for a device that cannot report its state.

This matters in scenes. A wake action means "the packet was sent"; it does not guarantee the following commands will land instantly. A PC that is just powering on may take tens of seconds before its network services accept commands. Build a delay into the scene if the next step talks to the machine you just woke.

Set it up

1

Enable WoL on the target machine

In BIOS/UEFI turn on Wake on LAN / PCIe device wake and turn off ErP or Deep Sleep. In the OS, set the NIC's "Wake on Magic Packet" option to Enabled.

2

Use a wired connection

Plug in an Ethernet cable. WiFi almost never survives sleep/shutdown. A USB-to-Ethernet adapter only works if it stays powered in standby and its driver supports WoL — many do not.

3

Allow the NIC to wake the computer

On Windows, enable "Allow this device to wake the computer" (and "Only allow a magic packet") in the adapter's power-management settings, and disable Fast Startup — otherwise shutdown (S5) wake will not work.

4

Give it a stable IP

A DHCP reservation in your router keeps the target reachable. Make sure AP/client isolation is off and the phone is on the same subnet.

5

Add the device in Remote97

Install from the download page, add a "Network wake" device, and enter its MAC address using the six-box input (no colons needed). You can edit the MAC later in the device details; scenes and panels pick up the change automatically.

Troubleshooting: it still will not wake

  • Nothing happens at all: confirm the machine can be woken by another WoL tool first — that isolates the PC's own settings from the app.
  • Works from sleep, not from shutdown: almost always Fast Startup, ErP power saving, or a USB NIC that loses power in S5.
  • Phone on WiFi, target on Ethernet: check that the router is not isolating wireless clients and that both are on the same subnet.
  • Sleep works but shutdown does not: that is a hardware/BIOS limitation on many laptops, not an app bug.
  • Need vendor-specific power commands instead: a serial-over-IP gateway or the device's own network protocol is the usual route.
