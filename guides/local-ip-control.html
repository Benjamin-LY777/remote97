How it works

Why local IP control beats cloud hubs

Most "smart" remotes route your commands through the vendor's servers. Remote97 talks to your gear directly on your home network — and that changes everything.

6 min read

Every device in a modern home theater already speaks a control language over IP: receivers listen on Telnet or HTTP, TVs and streamers answer UPnP/mDNS, Roku answers ECP. They don't need a middleman. Remote97 is the middleman removed.

The protocols, in plain English

  • SSDP / mDNS — discovery. "What's on my network?" Remote97 asks and devices answer.
  • Telnet / HTTP — command. "Set volume 40", "switch to HDMI 2". Denon, Marantz and many AVRs use this.
  • UPnP / SOAP — used by Sonos and others for playback and grouping.
  • ECP — Roku's control protocol.
  • MRP — Apple TV's remote protocol.

Three reasons local wins

1

Works offline

No internet? No problem. Your commands never leave the house, so a broadband outage can't take your remote down.

2

Private by design

What you watch and what you press stays on your network. There is no account, no telemetry, no cloud log of your habits.

3

No subscription

Cloud hubs charge monthly because they run servers. With no servers, there's nothing to bill you for.

Is it less capable? No. Direct IP control is actually faster and more reliable than round-tripping through a data center — and it keeps working when the vendor sunset their cloud.

What you need

  • Your phone and devices on the same WiFi
  • Devices with network control (anything from the last ~10 years generally qualifies)
  • That's it — no bridge, no hub, no account
