Control Denon & Marantz AVR from your phone

Replace the lost plastic remote — and the expensive control hub — with your phone. Remote97 talks to Denon and Marantz receivers over your local network.

6 min read · Works on Android, Windows & iOS

If your Denon or Marantz receiver is on the network but the handset remote is lost, dead, or buried in the couch, you don't need a replacement remote or a $500 control processor. Any phone on the same WiFi can drive it.

What you can control

  • Volume & mute — including relative and absolute levels across zones
  • Input / source — switch HDMI, TV, Blu-ray, Streaming, HEOS and more in one tap
  • Sound modes — Dolby, DTS, stereo, Pure Direct and others your model supports
  • Power & standby — on, off and eco standby
  • Multi-room (HEOS) — group and level HEOS zones where available

How it works (no cloud, no hub)

Local only. Denon and Marantz receivers speak a simple text protocol over Telnet (port 23) or HTTP on the local network. Remote97 sends commands directly to the receiver's IP address — nothing leaves your home, and it keeps working when the internet is down.

Steps

1

Install Remote97

Get it from Google Play, the App Store or Microsoft Store. No account, no setup wizard.

2

Put your phone on the same WiFi

The receiver and phone must be on the same local network. (Avoid VPN/tunnel apps that redirect traffic.)

3

Open Remote97 and let it discover

It scans with SSDP and mDNS and lists compatible receivers. Pick yours.

4

Control

Tap the receiver to adjust volume, switch inputs, change sound modes and power it on/off.

Which models are supported?

Essentially any Denon or Marantz AVR with an Ethernet or WiFi connection and network control — the same models that work with the manufacturer's own HEOS/AVR apps. That covers the X-series, A-series, SR-series and most network receivers from roughly the last decade.

Tips

  • Assign your receiver a reserved IP in your router so it never changes address.
  • Group the AVR with your TV in a "Movie night" scene so one tap powers and configures the whole setup.
  • Prefer a wired connection for the AVR if you ever see lag — WiFi is fine, but Ethernet is rock-solid.
